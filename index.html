Karde — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 September 2026 · Applies to the Karde Android app (com.karde.app)

Karde collects nothing. It has no account, no server, and no internet permission. Everything you put into it is encrypted and stays on your phone. We — the developers — cannot see your cards, your documents, your photos, your PIN, or anything else you store, because none of it ever reaches us.

1. Who this policy is from

Karde is published by Axumora. If you have a question about this policy or about the app, write to support@getgubae.com.

2. What Karde collects

Nothing. Karde does not collect, transmit, sell, share, or analyse any personal information. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, no crash reporting service, and no account system.

The app is built without the Android INTERNET permission. That is a technical guarantee rather than a promise: the operating system will refuse any network request the app attempts, so data cannot leave your device through Karde even if a bug or a future dependency tried to send it.

3. What you store, and where it lives

You may choose to enter card details, identity document details, private notes, and photographs of your documents. All of it is written only to Karde's private storage area on your own device.

WhatWhere it is kept
Card and document details, notes, photosEncrypted in an app-private database on your phone
Your Karde PINStored as a one-way hash, never in readable form
Security question answersStored as one-way hashes, never in readable form
Your settingsApp-private preferences on your phone

4. Encryption

Your entries are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The encryption key is generated inside your phone's hardware-backed Android Keystore and is marked so that it can never be exported from the device.

Using that key requires you to confirm your identity — your fingerprint, face, or device screen lock — every time. This is why Karde cannot open your vault unless your phone has a screen lock set.

One consequence is worth stating plainly: if you remove or reset your phone's screen lock, Android destroys that key. Your stored items become permanently unreadable, and nobody — including us — can recover them. An encrypted backup is the only thing that survives it.

5. Permissions Karde asks for

Karde asks for each of these at the moment it needs it, never all at once when the app first opens. Refusing any of them leaves the rest of the app working.

PermissionWhy
USE_BIOMETRIC To ask for your fingerprint or face before unlocking the app or revealing an item. The biometric check happens inside Android; Karde never receives your fingerprint or face data.
CAMERA To photograph a card or document, using a camera screen inside Karde so the picture never passes through another app. Asked for when you tap Take a photo, and at no other time. The photograph is encrypted into your vault on this device. If you refuse, Karde offers to use your phone's own camera app instead.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS To show you a reminder before a card or document expires. Asked for the first time Karde has a reminder to set. Every reminder is created and shown on this device; nothing about it is sent anywhere.
SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM To make a reminder arrive at the time it was set for rather than whenever Android next feels like it. It is used only for your own expiry reminders.
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED To put your reminders back in place after the phone restarts. Android discards pending reminders on restart, so without this a reminder you had set would silently never arrive.

That is the entire list of what Karde asks you for. It does not request internet access, contacts, location, your microphone, or access to your photo library.

Three further entries appear on Karde's Google Play permission list. Karde does not ask you for any of them; they come from the software libraries it is built with.

EntryWhat it is for
VIBRATE So an expiry reminder can buzz rather than arrive silently.
USE_FINGERPRINT The older form of the fingerprint permission. It is what makes fingerprint unlock work on Android 7 and 8, where the newer one does not yet exist.
WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE Declared by the camera library and limited to Android 9 and earlier. Karde writes only to its own private area; on Android 10 and later this entry does not apply at all.

6. Photographs of documents

A photograph you attach is encrypted together with the record it belongs to. Before it is stored, Karde strips every EXIF tag the original carried — including the GPS coordinates a phone normally writes into a picture, which on a photograph of an identity document would otherwise record where its owner lives.

7. Backups

Karde can write your whole vault to a single encrypted file. This happens only if you turn it on. You choose the passphrase and you choose the folder the file is kept in.

You can also switch on automatic backup, which keeps that one file up to date whenever something in your vault changes, so you do not have to remember to do it. It writes to the same folder you picked, replacing the same file each time.

Karde never uploads anything itself. It has no internet permission, so it cannot. It writes the file to the folder you chose, and nothing more. If the folder you chose belongs to a cloud service — Google Drive, for instance — then that service's own app syncs the file, under its own terms, exactly as it would for any other file you put there. The file is encrypted with your passphrase before Karde writes it, so the cloud service holds something it cannot read.

The passphrase is never stored and never leaves your device, which also means we cannot recover it for you. If you lose the passphrase, the backup file cannot be opened by anyone, including us.

Android's own automatic cloud backup is switched off for Karde (allowBackup="false"), so your vault is never copied to Google Drive.

8. Reminders

Karde can remind you before a card or document expires. The dates it uses are the ones you typed in yourself, every reminder is worked out and shown on this device, and nothing about them is sent anywhere.

A reminder deliberately names nothing. It tells you how many items are expiring, not which ones and not whose, because a notification appears on your lock screen where anyone standing nearby can read it.

Reminders rely on your phone letting Karde do a small amount of work in the background. Some phones — Samsung and Xiaomi especially — restrict that by default, and a reminder will not arrive until you allow it. Karde tells you when it can see this is the case, rather than letting you believe a reminder is coming when it is not.

9. Screenshots

Karde marks its windows as secure, so Android blocks screenshots and screen recording inside the app and hides its contents from the recent-apps preview.

10. Deleting your data

You are in full control and no request to us is necessary, because we hold nothing to delete. You can:

  • delete any single card or document from within the app;
  • use Settings → Erase vault to permanently destroy every card, document, photo, your PIN and your settings; or
  • uninstall Karde, which removes its private storage along with it.

Because nothing is stored in a cloud, any of these is final. Encrypted backup files you saved yourself are not affected — delete those wherever you put them.

11. Children

Karde is not directed at children and is not designed for or marketed to anyone under 13. It collects no information from anyone, children included.

12. Third parties

Karde contains no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking of any kind. No data is shared with any third party, because no data is gathered in the first place.

If you install Karde through Google Play, Google collects its own information about the installation under Google's privacy policy. That is between you and Google; it is outside Karde and outside our sight.
